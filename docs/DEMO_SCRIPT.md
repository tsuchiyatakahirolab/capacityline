# CapacityLine demo video — 2:44 target

The video must be public on YouTube or Vimeo, in English, and under three minutes. Record at 1440p or 1080p. Use browser zoom and cursor emphasis rather than a slide deck. Do not use copyrighted music or visible third-party trademarks.

## 0:00–0:14 — The operational problem

**Screen:** Recovery desk hero and 47-hour countdown.

**Narration:**

> A supplier outage has left a fictional automotive plant six thousand coolant pumps short. The production line stops in forty-seven hours. Risk software can flag the disruption—but it cannot tell this buyer which approved backup can actually commit capacity today.

## 0:14–0:31 — Who uses and pays

**Screen:** Point to the shortfall, downtime exposure, approved supplier list, and buyer policy.

**Narration:**

> CapacityLine is an AI supply recovery desk for the procurement team that owns this downtime exposure. It starts with suppliers the buyer is already allowed to contact and rules the buyer already controls: quantity, date, price, certification, origin, and decision authority.

## 0:31–0:48 — CALL-E is the core

**Screen:** Click Run recovery sprint; show Safe demo and Live CALL-E choices. Briefly cut to a six-second code/payload overlay showing `recipientResultSchema` and the CALL-E SDK call.

**Narration:**

> One recovery sprint creates parallel, goal-driven CALL-E conversations. The production path uses the official server SDK, a strict result schema, transcripts, evidence, and idempotency. This accelerated mode is fictional and makes no calls; live mode requires authorized E.164 numbers and explicit confirmation.

## 0:48–1:13 — Parallel commitments

**Screen:** Run the scenario. Let the five rows resolve.

**Narration:**

> CALL-E adapts to each conversation and returns comparable commitments—not just summaries. In seconds of demo time, two suppliers qualify, one can only supply four thousand a day late, one does not answer, and one looks perfect on quantity, date, and price.

## 1:13–1:35 — The decision that proves quality

**Screen:** Open Delta Fluidics. Highlight missing IATF 16949 and the evidence quote. Then close.

**Narration:**

> But the cheapest fast option cannot establish its required IATF certification. CapacityLine blocks it. The voice agent gathers facts; deterministic policy makes the classification; uncertainty fails closed. This is why the product is more than a parallel dialer.

## 1:35–2:04 — Evidence and human authority

**Screen:** Open Kanto Flow Systems. Show 7/7, evidence quote, respondent authority, transcript, and conditions. Click Approve RFQ handoff.

**Narration:**

> Kanto passes all seven guardrails. The buyer can inspect the exact supporting words, respondent authority, and freight condition before acting. A human approves an RFQ handoff. CapacityLine never places an order, promises payment, or forms a contract.

## 2:04–2:25 — The result

**Screen:** Close drawer; show KPI cards and approval banner. Open Commitment ledger.

**Narration:**

> The north-star metric is Time to First Qualified Fallback: twelve minutes and forty-one seconds in this scenario, versus an eight-hour synthetic manual baseline. Every outcome remains traceable, including no answer. The four-hundred-twenty-thousand-dollar exposure is a modeled scenario input—not claimed savings.

## 2:25–2:44 — Why this becomes a company

**Screen:** Open Supplier graph; end on logo and tagline.

**Narration:**

> Manufacturers operate it; procurement and operations leaders pay for it. Over time, each tenant builds a private commitment graph: who answers, who has authority, what they promise, and what they actually deliver. CapacityLine: call suppliers, secure capacity, keep the line moving.
